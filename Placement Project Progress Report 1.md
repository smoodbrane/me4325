**Subject:** Progress Report: Manufacturing Cell Setup for Low-Volume Production

## Work Completed

Since the placement project topic was confirmed, work has focused on defining the problem
and understanding the process the new manufacturing cells will need to support. The
project involves setting up manufacturing cells for a new low-volume production line,
following the DMAIC method. AT present, the product is moving towards production, but no cells
currently exist. The goal is a set of cells that meet output, quality and compliance targets
while making efficient use of space, equipment and operator time.

The product covers the design, setup and validation of these cells, but not wider site layout,
product design changes or increased volume planning. It draws on a wide range of knowledge
and involves real uncertainty, with several viable solutions to evaluate.

To understand what each cell will need to do I have:
- Drafted a control plan for the new line, capturing assembly steps and quality tests
- Reviewed existing assembly process documentation to identify the operations each cell
must perform
- Drafted tooling and fixture lists

## Work in Progress

I am currently corresponding with R&D to confirm the tests and tooling required. This will
establish which inspection and testing steps each cell must accommodate and what equipment
and fixtures need to be placed in each cell.

## Work Remaining

Once the required tests and tooling have been confirmed with R&D, the next stage is to
source and prepare the equipment the cells will need, and to plan the physical layout.
The main tasks are:
- **One 30 Sep 2026:** Visit site to assess the available space and plan layout
- **By Dec 2026:** Design and make any custom tooling and fixture not available
to purchase
- **By Jan 2027:** Finalise the proposed cell layout
- **By Feb 2027:**Conduct first production test, produce 100 units

## Complications or Issues Encountered

The main issue so far is the lack of physical models of the product. Without them it is
difficult to get a clear picture of each part required and how they fit together, which
limits how precisely tooling, fixtures and the cell layout can be planned. This will be resolved
once a small number of test versions are made within the next few weeks, Allowing the assembly
process to be examined directly.

A further constraint is that certain fixtures must be made by external contractors and will not
be available until February. As these fixtures are needed before production can begin, the first
production test cannot take place until they arrive.

Lastly, the product contains complex electronics that neither I nor the cell operators are familiar
with. Close cooperation with R&D will be needed to understand how these components are assembled, handled
and tested.
